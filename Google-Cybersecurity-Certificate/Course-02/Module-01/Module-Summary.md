# Module Summary: Security Domains

## Overview

Module 1 of **Play It Safe: Manage Security Risks** introduced security domains and foundational risk-management concepts. It explained how cybersecurity work can be organized into different areas and how threats, risks, and vulnerabilities can affect an organization.

## Key learning areas

### 1. CISSP's eight security domains

The module reviewed the focus of the eight domains in the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) framework. These domains organize cybersecurity knowledge into major areas of security practice.

### 2. Threats, risks, and vulnerabilities

- A **threat** is a potential cause of harm.
- A **vulnerability** is a weakness that a threat could exploit.
- A **risk** is the potential for an event to affect an organization's assets or its confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

Understanding how these ideas relate helps organizations decide what needs protection and where security improvements may be needed.

### 3. Ransomware

Ransomware is malicious software used to deny access to data, commonly by encrypting it, while attackers demand payment. A ransomware incident can interrupt business operations, affect data availability, and create financial and reputational damage.

### 4. Three layers of the web

The module introduced the surface web, deep web, and dark web as different layers or parts of the web. They are not interchangeable terms; the dark web is a small part of the deep web and typically requires special software or configurations to access.

### 5. NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF)

The module introduced the seven steps of the NIST RMF:

1. **Prepare** – Establish the activities needed to manage security and privacy risks.
2. **Categorize** – Categorize the information and systems based on their impact levels.
3. **Select** – Choose and document appropriate security controls.
4. **Implement** – Put the selected controls into operation.
5. **Assess** – Check whether the controls are implemented correctly and work as intended.
6. **Authorize** – An authorized official makes an informed decision about accepting system-related risks.
7. **Monitor** – Keep checking systems, controls, and changes to understand ongoing risk.

## Why this knowledge matters

Organizations need a structured way to identify risks and decide how to protect their systems and information. Understanding threats, vulnerabilities, ransomware, and the RMF provides a foundation for making informed security decisions.

## Key takeaway

Cybersecurity risk management is an ongoing process. Organizations identify what they need to protect, understand possible threats and weaknesses, select suitable controls, assess those controls, and continue monitoring their systems.
