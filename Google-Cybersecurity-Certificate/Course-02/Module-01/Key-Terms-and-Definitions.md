# Key Terms and Definitions

The following terms were introduced in Course 2, Module 1: **Security Domains**. Definitions are restated in simple language for revision.

| Term | Simple definition |
|---|---|
| **Assess** | The fifth step of the NIST RMF. Check whether selected security and privacy controls have been implemented correctly and work as intended. |
| **Authorize** | The sixth step of the NIST RMF. An authorized person makes and accepts the decision about the system's remaining security and privacy risks. |
| **Business continuity** | An organization's ability to keep essential operations running during and after a disruption. |
| **Categorize** | The second step of the NIST RMF. Categorize information and systems according to their security impact so suitable protections can be selected. |
| **External threat** | A possible source of harm that comes from outside an organization, such as an external attacker. |
| **Implement** | The fourth step of the NIST RMF. Put the selected security and privacy controls into practice. |
| **Internal threat** | A current or former employee, vendor, or trusted partner who may create a security risk through their access or actions. |
| **Monitor** | The seventh step of the NIST RMF. Keep observing systems, controls, and changes to identify new or changing risks. |
| **Prepare** | The first step of the NIST RMF. Establish the activities and responsibilities needed to manage security and privacy risks. |
| **Ransomware** | Malicious software that commonly encrypts an organization's data and demands payment in exchange for restoring access. |
| **Risk** | The possibility that a threat or event could affect an asset, including its confidentiality, integrity, or availability. |
| **Risk mitigation** | Actions taken to reduce the likelihood or impact of a risk. |
| **Security posture** | An organization's overall ability to protect its important systems, assets, and data and respond to changes or threats. |
| **Select** | The third step of the NIST RMF. Choose and document the security controls needed to protect a system. |
| **Shared responsibility** | The idea that everyone in an organization has a role in reducing risk and supporting physical and digital security. |
| **Social engineering** | A manipulation technique that takes advantage of human behavior or mistakes to obtain information, access, or other valuable resources. |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness in a system, process, or security measure that a threat could exploit. |

## Quick example: How the terms connect

Imagine a company employee receives a convincing fake email asking them to open an attachment.

- The email may be a **social engineering** attempt.
- The sender may be an **external threat**.
- An unpatched computer or weak email protection may be a **vulnerability**.
- A successful attack could create a **risk** to company data.
- **Risk mitigation** could include security updates, email filtering, backups, and employee awareness training.
- **Business continuity** planning can help the company continue essential work if an incident occurs.

## The seven NIST RMF steps in order

**Prepare → Categorize → Select → Implement → Assess → Authorize → Monitor**
