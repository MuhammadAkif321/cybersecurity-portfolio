# Module 3: Key Terms and Definitions

The following terms were introduced in **Protects Against Threats, Risks, and Vulnerabilities**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Asset** | Something an organization considers valuable and wants to protect, such as data, equipment, applications, or services. |
| **Availability** | The principle that information and systems should be accessible to authorized users when needed. |
| **Compliance** | Following applicable internal policies, standards, laws, and external regulations. |
| **Confidentiality** | Ensuring that only authorized people can access particular information or assets. |
| **Confidentiality, integrity, availability (CIA) triad** | A model used to think about the three important goals of information security: confidentiality, integrity, and availability. |
| **Hacktivist** | A person who uses hacking or digital attacks to pursue a political or social cause. |
| **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)** | A United States federal law that includes protections for certain health information. |
| **Integrity** | Ensuring that information is accurate, authentic, and reliable and has not been improperly changed. |
| **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** | A framework of guidance, standards, and best practices that organizations can use to manage cybersecurity risk. |
| **Privacy protection** | Safeguarding personal information against unauthorized access, use, or disclosure. |
| **Protected health information (PHI)** | Individually identifiable health information relating to a person's past, present, or future physical or mental health or healthcare. |
| **Security architecture** | The overall design of security components, tools, and processes used to protect an organization and its assets. |
| **Security controls** | Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks. |
| **Security ethics** | Principles and guidelines that help security professionals make responsible and appropriate decisions. |
| **Security frameworks** | Structured guidance used to organize security plans and help manage risks and threats to information and privacy. |
| **Security governance** | The leadership, policies, roles, and practices that guide and oversee an organization's security efforts. |
| **Sensitive personally identifiable information (SPII)** | A more sensitive category of personally identifiable information that requires stricter handling and protection. |

## Quick examples

- **Confidentiality:** Only the HR team can open confidential employee records.
- **Integrity:** A payroll record must not be changed without authorization.
- **Availability:** Employees must be able to access an essential business application when they need it.
- **Security control:** Multi-factor authentication helps reduce the risk of unauthorized account access.
- **Compliance:** A company follows the privacy and security requirements that apply to its operations.
- **Asset:** A customer database is an asset because the organization depends on it and must protect it.

## Revision reminder

The CIA triad has three separate goals: keep information private (**confidentiality**), keep it correct (**integrity**), and keep it accessible to authorized users (**availability**). A framework gives structured guidance; a control is a safeguard used to reduce risk.
