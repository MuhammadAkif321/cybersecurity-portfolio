# Module 2 Summary: The Evolution of Cybersecurity

## What I learned

This module explained how major security incidents influenced the cybersecurity industry. It introduced early malware examples such as the **Brain virus** and the **Morris worm**. These incidents helped demonstrate how malicious code can spread and disrupt computer systems. Some modern attacks use techniques or ideas that resemble earlier forms of malware, so understanding the history of attacks can help security professionals recognize changing threats.

The module also discussed more recent incidents, including the **LoveLetter attack** and the **Equifax data breach**. These examples highlighted the impact that attacks can have on individuals and organizations, including exposure of sensitive information, disruption, loss of trust, and financial costs.

Another major topic was **social engineering**. Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information, providing access, or taking actions that benefit an attacker. The module introduced several forms of phishing and impersonation, along with the motives that may influence threat actors.

Finally, the module introduced the **eight CISSP security domains**. These domains provide a way to group and understand different areas of information security. They help organize the broad responsibilities and knowledge areas involved in protecting an organization.

## Why this knowledge matters

Cybersecurity is an evolving field. Studying historical incidents helps explain why security practices changed and why organizations need to keep adapting. Understanding social engineering is especially important because people can be targeted even when technical systems have security protections.

## Key takeaway

Learning from past attacks helps us understand present-day threats. Social engineering shows that people are part of the security environment, while the CISSP domains provide a structure for understanding the different areas of security work.
