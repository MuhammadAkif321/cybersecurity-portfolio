# Module 2: Key Terms and Definitions

The following terms were introduced in **The Evolution of Cybersecurity**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Adversarial artificial intelligence (AI)** | The use or manipulation of AI and machine-learning systems to make attacks more effective or efficient. |
| **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** | A type of email-based attack in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick someone into transferring money or sharing information. |
| **CISSP** | Certified Information Systems Security Professional, a widely recognized information security certification awarded by ISC2. |
| **Computer virus** | Malicious code designed to interfere with computer operations and potentially damage data or software. |
| **Cryptographic attack** | An attack that targets cryptographic methods or systems used to protect communication and information. |
| **Hacker** | A person who uses technical skills to access or work with computer systems, networks, or data. The word does not automatically mean a criminal; context matters. |
| **Malware** | Software designed to harm, disrupt, exploit, or gain unauthorized access to devices or networks. |
| **Password attack** | An attempt to obtain or use passwords to gain unauthorized access to protected systems, accounts, or data. |
| **Phishing** | A technique that uses digital messages or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information or installing malicious software. |
| **Physical attack** | A security incident that affects a physical environment as well as, or instead of, digital systems. |
| **Physical social engineering** | An attack in which someone impersonates an employee, customer, or vendor to gain unauthorized access to a physical location. |
| **Social engineering** | Manipulating human behaviour or exploiting human error to obtain information, access, or valuables. |
| **Social media phishing** | An attack that uses information gathered from social media to make a targeted deception more convincing. |
| **Spear phishing** | A targeted phishing attack aimed at a particular person or group, often using a message that appears to come from a trusted source. |
| **Supply-chain attack** | An attack that targets a supplier, service provider, or component in order to compromise the systems or organizations that depend on it. |
| **USB baiting** | An attack in which a malicious USB device is left where someone may find and plug it into a computer. |
| **Virus** | Another name for a computer virus. |
| **Vishing** | Voice-based social engineering that attempts to obtain sensitive information or impersonate a trusted source. |
| **Watering hole attack** | An attack in which a threat actor compromises a website frequently visited by a particular group of people. |

## Quick examples

- **BEC:** An attacker pretends to be a company executive and emails the finance team with fake payment instructions.
- **Spear phishing:** An employee receives a convincing message that refers to their actual job role and asks them to open a malicious attachment.
- **Vishing:** A caller pretends to be technical support and asks a user to reveal a one-time password.
- **USB baiting:** Someone finds an unknown USB drive in a car park and plugs it into a work computer.
- **Watering hole:** Attackers compromise a website commonly visited by employees of a particular organization.

## Revision reminder

**Phishing** is the broad use of deceptive digital communication. **Spear phishing** is more specifically targeted. **Vishing** uses voice communication. **BEC** commonly uses impersonation to pursue financial or information-related goals.
