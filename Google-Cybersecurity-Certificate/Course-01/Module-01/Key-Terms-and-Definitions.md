# Module 1: Key Terms and Definitions

The following terms were introduced in **Welcome to the Exciting World of Cybersecurity**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cybersecurity (security)** | The practice of protecting networks, devices, people, and data from unauthorized access or criminal exploitation while supporting confidentiality, integrity, and availability. |
| **Cloud security** | Protecting assets stored in cloud services through secure configuration and access limited to authorized users. |
| **Internal threat** | A security risk caused by a current or former employee, external vendor, or trusted partner who has access to an organization or its information. |
| **Network security** | The practice of protecting an organization's network infrastructure from unauthorized access and misuse. |
| **Personally identifiable information (PII)** | Information that can be used to identify, or help infer the identity of, a person. |
| **Security posture** | An organization's overall ability to manage the protection of its important assets and data and respond to changes in its risk environment. |
| **Sensitive personally identifiable information (SPII)** | A type of PII that requires stricter handling and protection because its exposure could create greater harm. |
| **Technical skills** | Skills that require knowledge of specific tools, technologies, procedures, or policies. |
| **Threat** | A circumstance or event that could negatively affect an asset. |
| **Threat actor** | A person or group that presents a security risk or carries out a threat. |
| **Transferable skills** | Skills gained in one area that can also be applied in other roles or careers. |

## Quick examples

- **Cloud security:** Restricting access to company files stored in a cloud service so only approved employees can open them.
- **Internal threat:** An employee intentionally copying confidential customer information without permission.
- **PII:** A person's name combined with other identifying information.
- **Threat actor:** A criminal group attempting to steal an organization's data.
- **Transferable skill:** Clear communication helps a security professional explain a risk to a non-technical manager.

## Revision reminder

PII is information that can identify a person. SPII is a more sensitive category of PII and therefore needs stricter protection. A threat is a possible source of harm; a threat actor is the person or group behind a harmful action.
